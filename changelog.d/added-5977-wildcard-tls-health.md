- The hub now watches the fleet wildcard certificate on every cluster that serves its spokes from one ([#5977](https://github.com/hivecommons/hive/issues/5977)). Opting a cluster in with `wildcard_tls_secret` means provisioned spoke Ingresses carry no `tls:` block of their own, so a single certificate stands behind every hosted dashboard there — roughly 263 hostnames across the two clusters in the issue — and a renewal that silently fails takes all of them down at once instead of one. Each cluster-health build now reads the ingress-nginx default SSL flag and asserted secret (read-only, and only on opted-in clusters) and reports it as `wildcard_tls` in `/api/cluster-health`, rendered on the fleet-health cluster row: `expiring` under 21 days, which means renewal is overdue rather than merely upcoming, since cert-manager begins renewing a 90-day certificate at 30 days out; plus `expired`, `missing`, `domain_mismatch`, `not_served` and `unreadable`. This is also the first check anywhere on the opt-in itself: `wildcard_tls_secret` is an operator assertion the provisioner cannot verify — it has to decide without a cluster round-trip — so "the flag is set and the secret is not there", "ingress-nginx is not serving it as the default certificate", and "the certificate does not actually carry `*.<cluster domain>`", were all silent until someone opened a dashboard and got ingress-nginx's self-signed default. A healthy certificate renders nothing, and a cluster the hub cannot reach reports nothing rather than a reassuring `ok`.
